Wireless Mic Support

Wireless Mic runs on iPhone with iOS 17 or later. The app uses the audio inputs and routes made available by iOS.

Need help?

Email info@sdilejslunce.cz. Include your iPhone model, iOS version, microphone or receiver model, connection type, and what the app showed as the active input. Attach audio only if you are comfortable sharing it.

Before an important recording

Connect and power on the microphone or receiver before opening the recorder. Confirm the input name shown in Wireless Mic, make a short test recording, play it back through headphones, and leave enough free storage. Charge the iPhone and microphone equipment for long sessions.

If the wrong microphone is used

Open the microphone chooser and select an input offered by iOS. If an accessory is missing, reconnect it, unlock the iPhone, and refresh the list. Bluetooth profiles and USB accessories can expose different capabilities. Wireless Mic cannot force an input that iOS does not make available, and it does not infer brand-specific features.

Clipping, quiet sound, or dropouts

Clipping means the incoming signal reached an unsafe level; lower gain on the microphone or receiver and repeat the test. For quiet audio, move the microphone closer or raise hardware gain gradually. Radio interference, a loose cable, low accessory power, or an iOS route change can cause dropouts. The app records the signal it receives and marks detected capture problems, but it cannot reconstruct sound that never reached the iPhone.

If recording is interrupted

Wireless Mic writes verified segments during capture. After an interruption or unexpected app termination, reopen the app and look for the recovered-recording card. You can review the audio already saved, keep it as the finished take, or continue the logical session. Continuing creates a separate take and clearly preserves the unrecorded gap; it never appends over or rewrites the recovered audio.

Low storage, low battery, and closing the app

The recorder warns when available storage or iPhone battery is low. Stop and save before storage is exhausted, and connect power for long recordings. If iOS ends the app or the phone loses power, only audio already committed to storage can be recovered. Do not force-quit the app while it says it is preparing or saving.

Files, edits, and backups

Recordings and their review information are stored locally. Edits are non-destructive and do not alter original audio. Exports create separate files. For an additional copy, choose an archive destination in Files and verify the copy in that destination. Selecting an iCloud Drive or third-party Files folder does not itself prove that its provider has finished uploading.

Notifications, location, and wrist controls

Interruption notifications are optional. Recording location is also optional and is not embedded in shared audio. Shortcuts, the Action Button, and supported system controls can request recording actions, but always confirm the recorder’s visible state before relying on a remote command.
