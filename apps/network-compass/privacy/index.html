Privacy Policy

Effective October 11, 2026 · Network Compass 1.0

Network Compass is developed by Adam Zemančík. Local network discoveries and your saved library are processed on your device. There are no advertising or analytics SDKs and no tracking. Internet diagnostics contact external services, and the optional server companion stores the information described below.

Local diagnostics and saved data

Device names, addresses, services, scan history, favorites, notes, network profiles, diagnostic history, request presets, Bluetooth observations, NFC records and imported PCAP/PCAPNG captures are processed locally. The app does not automatically upload your local network inventory or capture files to the developer. The Safari share extension prepares a target locally; it does not automatically run a check. The server-health widget shares its configuration and public health snapshot with the app on the same device.

Saved HTTP request presets can contain URLs, request bodies and non-authentication headers. MQTT credentials are kept only for the current session. The in-app HTTP journal stays in memory and redacts authentication/cookie headers and request query values. Remote responses and exported files can still contain sensitive information.

Internet tools and third-party services

When you run an internet check, the selected host and service receive your request and the public source IP needed to respond. DNS-over-HTTPS uses Cloudflare; registration checks use rdap.org and the relevant RDAP registry; network ownership checks use Team Cymru DNS through Cloudflare; public-IP lookup uses ipify; transfer tests use Cloudflare. Your device’s configured DNS resolver may also receive ordinary DNS requests. HTTP, TLS, ping, traceroute, ports, protocol and iPerf tools contact the targets or servers you choose.

These providers operate under their own privacy policies. Network Compass does not use them for advertising or cross-app tracking. Do not put passwords or other secrets into a target URL or diagnostic input.

Optional server companion

Connecting the companion creates a random pairing/device identifier, a private authentication credential and the device label you enter. The credential is stored in the iOS Keychain; the server stores a hash of it. No personal account or email address is required. The default companion is operated by the developer at playground.servix.cz. If you select a different companion, that server’s operator controls its data handling.

Checks you explicitly run on the companion send the chosen public targets and relevant inputs, including URL query strings, selected ports, DNS records, expected response values and monitoring settings. The server stores diagnostic jobs, results, timing and performance observations, monitor configuration, and incidents linked to your pairing identifier. It does not receive your local network scan or private capture file automatically.

The server uses a hash of your public source IP (or IPv6 network prefix) for connection and usage limits. A UDP quality test also temporarily stores your numeric source IP and the observed public IP and port to match the test packets. These addresses are used for networking and abuse prevention, not location profiling.

If you enable push alerts, the companion stores your Apple push token, associates it with your pairing, and sends that token and a monitor-alert payload to Apple’s push notification service. Alerts may display target names and incident details on your lock screen according to your iOS settings. Push is optional.

Server retention and deletion

Diagnostic jobs, observations and closed incidents are retained for up to seven days in the active service. Active monitor settings and unresolved incidents remain until removed or the incident closes. Pairing records remain while in use; inactive pairings without active monitors may be removed after 90 days. Rate-limit records expire after up to one day. UDP test sessions expire after a short test window and are deleted by cleanup or cancellation.

Pause or delete a monitor to stop its scheduled checks. Turn off server push to remove the registered push token. In the companion screen, revoke/disconnect the device to delete its pairing and associated server data. Deleted data expires from rotating backups within seven days. Deleting the iPhone app alone does not revoke an active server pairing or stop its monitors; disconnect it first, or contact support for help.

Location, nearby phones and hardware permissions

Location permission is used for Wi-Fi identity and for location diagnostics you explicitly start. Live position, accuracy and heading readings stay in memory and clear when you leave the screen or background the app. A location snapshot you choose to share includes coordinates. The developer does not automatically receive your location.

Local Network permission enables discovery and communication with selected local devices. Bluetooth permission enables nearby Bluetooth LE discovery, selected service interactions and test advertising. NFC sessions read or write supported tags only when started by you. Nearby phone ranging exchanges temporary interaction tokens over an encrypted connection after an accepted connection. Ranging readings stay in memory; your coordinates are not sent to the other phone.

You can manage these permissions and notifications in iOS Settings. Some features need compatible hardware and appropriate permission.

Sharing, backup and your choices

Reports, CSV/JSON exports, snapshots and capture exports go only to the destination you choose through the system share sheet or Files. They may include network/device names, addresses, coordinates in a location snapshot, headers, response metadata or packet content. Review the file before sharing. iOS and the chosen storage provider control device backups and synchronization under your settings.

You can delete local scan history, saved results and other stored items in the app. Deleting the app removes its app-controlled local files, subject to iOS backup/restore behavior; Keychain credentials and previously exported copies may remain. Remove exported files and backups at their destination separately.

Support and this website

If you contact support, we receive your email address, message and any attachments you choose to send, and use them to respond and resolve your request. We keep that correspondence while needed for the request and related support records. The app does not automatically attach diagnostics. Please omit credentials and private network or personal data unless needed and you choose to share it.

These pages are hosted by GitHub Pages. GitHub may process technical request information under GitHub’s Privacy Statement. The pages contain no added analytics, advertising scripts or cookies. Apple separately handles App Store, TestFlight, push notifications and other system services under its own terms.

Privacy requests and changes

For privacy questions, or to request access, correction or deletion of information held by the developer, email info@sdilejslunce.cz. Include your pairing/device identifier if available, but never your authentication token. We may need to verify that a request concerns your data. Applicable law may provide additional rights and the right to contact a data protection authority. This policy will be updated when the app’s data practices change.
